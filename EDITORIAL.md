**Thesis.** In this frozen small model, matched clause retrieval is governed more clearly by answer position and lexical competition than by context amount: end placement helps, naive word-overlap pruning strengthens the decoy, and removing that retained decoy reverses the failure, while the measured length effect is unresolved.

**Significance.** The result informs builders of offline small-model document QA deciding whether to shorten prompts with lexical filters: a cheap filter needs a decoy-resistant comparison before deployment, and the observed end-position gain is diagnostic rather than an automatic remedy.

**Originality.** Unlike NoLiMa's demonstration that literal overlap makes long-context tests too easy, this study uses paired synthetic clauses on a frozen laptop-scale open model to show that the same overlap can make keyword pruning select a competing answer and to diagnose the failure by removing only that competitor.
